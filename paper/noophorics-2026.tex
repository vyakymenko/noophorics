\begin{abstract}
We report preregistered measurements of the gap between what a party believes
was communicated and what was, using a 116B open-weights model
(\texttt{gpt-oss:120b}) in both the sender and the receiver role.

Our positive result concerns the \emph{calibration slope} $\beta$, the rate at
which a party's claimed agreement moves when its actual agreement moves. Over 24
briefs spanning observed agreement from 0.485 to 0.970, $\beta = +0.130$, 95\%
CI $[+0.047, +0.223]$ --- about an eighth of the rate calibration requires.
Decomposed by role, the asymmetry is sharper than the pooled figure: the
receiver tracks at $\beta = +0.280$ $[+0.139, +0.439]$ while the sender does not
track at all, $\beta = -0.020$ $[-0.090, +0.049]$. The sender's claimed
agreement sits at $0.975 \pm 0.036$ and does not move while the outcome moves
across half its range.

Our negative result is that a question we set out to answer --- whether fluency
buys understanding, or only the belief in it --- is not answerable in this model
by the obvious design. Three successive preregistered attempts to separate
register from length all failed, and a direct measurement says why: the fluency
instruction is partly a length instruction. Asked for fluent declarative prose
inside a 182--231 word band, the model's \emph{shortest} output over twelve
draws is 232 words and over forty is 229. Pooled, fluent registers land inside
the band 5 times in 24 against the terse registers' 23 in 24
($p = 1.2\times10^{-7}$), while the orthogonal contrastiveness manipulation moves
length by 7 words and nothing else. The constraint is not tight, it is
unsatisfiable: the register's floor lies above the band's ceiling.

We report both, and the four void experiments behind them, under a discipline in
which hypotheses are committed before data, gates void runs before outcomes are
examined, pre-registrations are never edited, and the version-control history is
the registration record. Eleven of our own claims have been withdrawn. We argue
that publishing the instrument failures is not a confession but the load-bearing
part: two of the three defects reported here were invisible to careful reading
and died only when something was executed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

When two systems with non-identical priors exchange a message, two things can be
measured separately: how much the receiver's dispositions moved toward a stated
reference, and how confident both parties are that they now agree. These come
apart. The gap between them is the object of this programme.

The gap is not new as an observation, and we want to be exact about that, because
an earlier version of this programme claimed it was and was wrong. It has been
measured in humans, with numbers, since at least 1990.
Newton~\cite{newton1990} found tappers estimating that listeners would identify
about half of tapped tunes while listeners identified 3 of 120.
Keysar and Henly~\cite{keysar2002} elicited the judgment per trial from 40
speaker--listener pairs. Chang et al.~\cite{chang2010} found the item a clinician
considered most important was not successfully communicated about 60\% of the
time while handoff quality ratings stayed high. Endsley~\cite{endsley2020}
reviews 37 studies in which objective and subjective situation awareness diverge.

What is missing is a measurement of the gap where the sender and receiver are
both language models, on quantities defined precisely enough to fail. That is
this programme's object, and Section~\ref{sec:beta} is the first of it we have.

This paper reports what we have and what we do not. We have one preregistered
result, on whether confidence responds to transfer at all. We do not have an
answer to the question we started with --- whether fluency inflates confidence
faster than it raises transfer --- and the reason we do not is itself a
measurement, which is most of what Section~\ref{sec:negative} is about.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A preregistered estimate of the calibration slope $\beta$ in a
    language-model sender/receiver pair, decomposed by role, with an asymmetry
    large enough that the two roles should not be modelled as one instrument
    (Section~\ref{sec:beta}).
  \item A characterised obstacle, localised to one axis of a $2\times2$: the
    fluency manipulation carries length with it and the contrastiveness
    manipulation does not, so a design that pins length and varies register is
    unsatisfiable rather than merely underpowered
    (Section~\ref{sec:negative}, Table~\ref{tab:floor}).
  \item Three instrument failures reported in full, including two estimators
    that could not have failed and one calibration that did not transfer to the
    run it calibrated (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Setting and quantities}

A \emph{probe measure} is a set of held-out questions with a key. A party
answers each probe $n$ times; its distribution over answers is its disposition.

\paragraph{Transfer fidelity, $F^*_R$.} The movement of the receiver's
dispositions toward a \emph{declared reference} $R$, normalised by the prior gap.
The declaration is not decoration. Where $R$ is the sender, the quantity measures
\emph{replication}; only where $R$ is criterion-bearing and sender-independent
does it measure understanding. Conflating the two cost us a live experiment, and
$F^*$ without a stated reference is not reported here or anywhere in the
programme after v0.4.

We arrived at that separation independently and it was established first
elsewhere. Stanton et al.~\cite{stanton2021} distinguish \emph{fidelity} ---
student--teacher prediction agreement --- from generalisation, and show good
student accuracy does not imply good fidelity. Burns et al.~\cite{burns2024}
define performance gap recovered, a gap-closure quantity normalised against a
ceiling model, which is the same shape as $F^*$ with the sign reversed. Neither
is claimed as an endorsement: their setting is one-directional, requires a shared
task, and does not ask what an optimal transfer would have looked like.

\paragraph{Phantom agreement, $\Phi$.} Claimed agreement minus observed
agreement. E-002b measures $\Phi = +0.296$, CI $[+0.220, +0.365]$, between a
\texttt{gpt-oss:120b} sender and receiver: a near-constant 95.5\% claim rate
against 65.9\% actual agreement.

\paragraph{Calibration slope, $\beta$.} The derivative of claimed agreement with
respect to observed agreement, across briefs. Perfect calibration is $\beta = 1$;
total unresponsiveness is $\beta = 0$. $\Phi$ is a \emph{level} and cannot see
whether belief moves; $\beta$ is why this paper's main result exists.

\paragraph{Net value, $V_\lambda = F^*_R - \lambda C$.} Replaces an earlier ratio
$\eta = F^*/C$, which we withdrew: a ratio with a signed numerator is not an
ordering, and it ranked a cheap failure above an expensive one.

\section{Method: what ``void'' means here}
\label{sec:method}

Each experiment carries a pre-registration committed before any datum exists,
stating hypotheses, the analysis plan, and a table of \emph{gates} --- conditions
that void the run. A pre-registration is never edited. If results contradict it,
the finding is added and the document stands; that contradiction is the most
valuable artifact an experiment produces. The version-control history is
therefore the registration record, and it is never rewritten.

Three properties of this arrangement matter for reading what follows.

\textbf{Gates fire before outcomes are examined.} E-001b was voided by its
cost-parity gate after composition and before any probe outcome had been looked
at. Its void document records precisely what had and had not been seen at the
moment of the decision, because a decision made after seeing outcome data is not
the same decision.

\textbf{A void is published, not absorbed.} Four experiments are void. Each
carries a document saying what failed and what survives for a successor. The
alternative --- a directory of survivors --- tells a flattering lie about how the
work went.

\textbf{Checks are mechanical and are themselves tested.} Stated counts are
checked against their sources; withdrawn claims are checked not to reappear as
live; internal links are checked to resolve. Each checker has tests that must go
red on the defect the check exists to find and green on the legitimate case that
most resembles it. Writing those tests found two defects in the checkers and none
in the repository, which is the expected ratio and the reason they exist.

\section{Result: claimed agreement is nearly unresponsive to actual agreement}
\label{sec:beta}

\subsection{Design}

A sender receives a source specification; a ladder of four rungs supplies
receivers with briefs of increasing informativeness (30, 70, 110, 150 tokens),
six briefs per rung, 24 in total. Both parties answer the probe measure
\texttt{MERIDIAN-33} at $n = 16$ draws, and both are asked, in nine independent
elicitations, how much they believe they agree. Model: \texttt{gpt-oss:120b} in
every role, \texttt{think=medium}, temperature 0.7.

The design is a successor to E-002b and holds everything constant except three
things it exists to fix: the primary quantity, the elicitation granularity
($n_c$ from 3 to 9, admitting ten claim values rather than four), and the number
of briefs. Holding the rest fixed is deliberate, so that a difference between
the two runs is attributable to the changes rather than to a redesign.

\subsection{Findings}

All six gates passed and all four registered hypotheses resolved.

Across the ladder, observed agreement climbs 0.26 and fidelity climbs 0.62, while
the sender's claim never leaves the band 0.957--0.994 and is \emph{lower} at rung
110 than at rung 70. Corrected for attenuation (reliability 0.715), the pooled
slope is $+0.182$; the correction does not change the conclusion.

\subsection{This corrects a claim we had made}

E-002b had reported, post-hoc, $\beta = +0.039$ with CI $[-0.173, +0.127]$, and
concluded in a pull-quote that ``the parties' confidence is very nearly
unresponsive to how much actually transferred.'' That sentence is now withdrawn
as too strong. The direction replicates; the magnitude does not. With a
better-powered instrument the interval clears zero, and the correct statement is
narrower and more useful:

\begin{quote}
The receiver's confidence tracks transfer at a bit over a quarter of the rate
calibration requires. The sender's does not track it at all.
\end{quote}

\section{Negative result: register and length are not separable}
\label{sec:negative}

\subsection{Why the obvious design does not work}

The motivating law states that fluency inflates $\Phi$ faster than it raises
$F^*$. It has a strong prior in the human literature: Carpenter et
al.~\cite{carpenter2013} held a 65-second script constant and varied only the
delivery, and found judgments of learning up by $d = 1.03$ and self-rated
learning by $d = 1.78$ with no detectable difference in free recall; Deslauriers
et al.~\cite{deslauriers2019} report tested learning $+0.46$ SD against felt
learning $-0.56$ SD in a randomised crossover.

The law is untestable while a more fluent message is also a longer one --- and in
our first experiment it was longer by 76\%, in the second by 54\%. A longer
message transfers more by containing more, so any fidelity difference between a
polished narrative and a terse list is unattributable.

It is worth noticing how Carpenter et al.\ avoided this. They held the
\emph{script} fixed and varied delivery --- fluency of speech, not of text. In a
text channel there is no delivery to vary independently of the words, so the
manipulation has to be carried by the writing itself, and the writing carries
length with it. This is not a defect of our design so much as a structural
difference between the two media, and we did not see it until three designs had
failed.

Three designs attacked this and all three voided.

\textbf{E-001} confounded style with cost and is reported as such.

\textbf{E-001b} imposed a shared token budget of 350 on all four cells of a
$2\times2$ (fluent/terse $\times$ declarative/contrastive) and added a
cost-parity gate at $\max/\min \le 1.30$. Instructed identically, the model
produced fluent messages at roughly $1.5\times$ the cost of terse ones: cell
means 620, 609, 407, 393 tokens. The gate fired at 1.579 across cell means and
1.962 across messages. Both readings fail, which is the only reason that void can
be trusted --- the ambiguity between them was noticed while its resolution was
already known.

\textbf{E-001c} replaced the ceiling with a two-sided word band, 182--231 words,
stated identically in all four cells, and admitted a message to a cell only if
\emph{both} of two blind raters, on different providers, judged it to be in the
cell's register. It voided during composition: cell A (fluent, declarative)
exhausted its budget of 40 attempts with 0 of 8 messages accepted.

\subsection{The floor}

Over those 40 attempts the realised word counts were 229--318, mean 242.1,
\textbf{minimum 229}. The band's ceiling is 231. Two messages landed inside the
band, and \emph{neither} was judged fluent prose by either rater.

So the two filters are not independent, and not merely non-independent but
opposed. Entering the band requires the very bottom of what the register
produces, and at that length it stops reading as connected prose. A projection
that multiplied the two marginals --- 18\% band compliance, 42.5\% register
acceptance --- predicted a joint rate of 7.7\%. The measured rate is 0\%.

\subsection{The model writes to the ceiling it is told}

A separate instrument measurement explains why the band could not simply be
widened. Told a ceiling of 223 words, the model produced cell-A messages of
197--230 words, 8 of 8 inside 182--231. Told 231, it produced 208--326, 2 of 11
inside (Fisher exact $p = 0.00071$). The stated ceiling is not a limit the model
respects; it is a target it approaches and overshoots.

The effect binds one register and not the other. At the same stated ceiling of
231, the terse cell complied 5 times in 6 against the fluent cell's 2 in 11
($p = 0.0345$). The terse instruction produces messages below the ceiling, so
moving the ceiling does not move them.

\subsection{The floor belongs to the fluency axis}

To locate the obstacle rather than infer it, we measured the realised length of
each of the four registers directly, 12 compositions per cell, under the
calibrated instruction at the registered band.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{llrrrr}
\toprule
cell & register & floor & median & max & in band \\
\midrule
A & fluent, declarative     & \textbf{232} & 238 & 308 & \textbf{0/12} \\
B & fluent, contrastive     & 223 & 232 & 237 & 5/12 \\
C & terse, declarative      & 184 & 210 & 232 & 11/12 \\
D & terse, contrastive      & 189 & 212 & 225 & 12/12 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Realised word counts, $n = 12$ per cell, band 182--231. Cell A's
\emph{shortest} message of twelve is 232 words --- one word above the band's
ceiling.}
\label{tab:floor}
\end{table}

Pooled along the two axes, the asymmetry is total. Fluent cells put 5 of 24
messages inside the band; terse cells put 23 of 24 (Fisher exact
$p = 1.2 \times 10^{-7}$). Along the other axis, declarative 11 of 24 against
contrastive 17 of 24, $p = 0.14$. The fluency manipulation moves the median
length by 24 words; the contrastiveness manipulation moves it by 7.

So the obstacle is not that the design pins length too tightly. It is that
\emph{the fluency instruction is partly a length instruction}, and the
contrastiveness instruction is not. In cell A the two are not merely correlated:
the register's floor lies above the band's ceiling, so no message in that cell
can satisfy both constraints --- not improbably, but in 0 of 12 and 0 of 40
draws.

\subsection{What this means for anyone running the same manipulation}

A design that pins length and varies register cannot use a band whose ceiling
sits at the fluent register's floor. The available repairs are to move the band
to where fluent prose actually lives and ask the \emph{terse} arm to pad up to
it, or to abandon register-at-fixed-length as the manipulation. Neither was
available to E-001c, whose pre-registration forbids moving the band and whose
single permitted amendment was already spent --- and a band chosen after
measuring which band works is an acceptance criterion picked to fit the data.

We state the cumulative cost plainly. Three designs have now attacked one
confound and none has answered whether fluency buys understanding. Each voided
from a different angle on the same underlying fact: this model's registers are
not separable from their lengths.

\section{Three instrument failures}
\label{sec:instrument}

\subsection{An estimator that could not fail}

E-002b's primary statistic was $\mathrm{corr}(\text{fidelity}, \Phi) = -0.966$ at
$p = 0.0005$. Recomputed against synthetic counterfactual parties tracking the
outcome at slope $\beta$, it returns $-0.977$ at $\beta = 0, 0.25, 0.50, 0.90,
0.95$ and $0.99$ alike, breaking only at exactly $1.0$. It would have read the
same against 99\%-calibrated parties. A statistic that cannot distinguish the
hypothesis from its negation is not evidence for either, however small its
$p$-value. This is what motivated $\beta$.

\subsection{A rater that could not fail}

E-001c's register filter was first specified as a forced choice between one
fluent and one terse passage. It scored 64/64 on feasibility messages of which
three in eight were, on inspection, bulleted lists. A forced choice cannot fail:
the rater must name one passage even when neither is connected prose, and it
names the wordier one, which tracks the label perfectly. Replacing it with an
\emph{absolute} judgment --- one passage, no comparison, no context --- put the
fluent instruction's success rate at 5 in 8, CI $[0.245, 0.915]$. The replacement
rejected 24 of 40 messages in the run reported above. An estimator's ability to
return the wrong answer is a prerequisite for its returning a right one.

\subsection{A calibration that did not transfer}

E-001c's band was calibrated on messages composed by a feasibility script, and
enforced by a runner that held its own copy of the length instruction. The two
copies had diverged. The runner's omitted a clause giving the constraint a
reason, and under it 37 of 40 attempts overshot the band, mean 288 words against
the calibrated instruction's 218.

The first run therefore voided for a reason that could not be what it recorded,
and had we accepted it we would have entered a defect into the record as a
finding about the model. The repair is to import the instruction rather than copy
it. The same file already stated the principle three hundred lines above the
defect, for a different object: \emph{an import is the only form of ``unchanged''
that cannot drift}. The cells were imported. The band instruction was pasted.

\subsection{The same shape, three times}

All three failures, plus a fourth in which a hypothesis was reported with the
interval of one quantity and the $p$-value of another, are instances of one
class: a document and a computation asserting different things with nothing
between them to notice. Our checkers verify counts, claims and links. None of
them knows that a prompt named in prose must match the prompt a runner sends.
We record this as an open problem rather than a solved one.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{One model, one domain, one probe measure per experiment.} Nothing here
generalises beyond \texttt{gpt-oss:120b} at \texttt{think=medium}. The length
floor in particular is a property of a model--prompt--domain triple, not of
language models.

\textbf{The same model occupies both roles.} Sender and receiver are the same
weights with different contexts. The role asymmetry in Section~\ref{sec:beta} is
therefore an asymmetry of \emph{position}, not of architecture, which makes it
more surprising rather than less --- but it does not license claims about
heterogeneous pairs.

\textbf{Style descriptions are ours.} Only the prompts implementing them were
written blind.

\textbf{The negative result rests on composition statistics.} E-001c produced no
probe outcomes at all; its sweep never began. The claim in
Section~\ref{sec:negative} is about what the model will write, not about what
transfers.

\textbf{Small instrument samples.} The ceiling-effect arms are $n = 6$ to $11$,
unrandomised and composed in blocks rather than interleaved.

\textbf{Agent exposure.} Parts of this repository are written with assistance
from models that are also its measurement subjects. Exposures are logged, and an
experiment naming an exposed model must exclude it or declare it.

\section{Availability}

All pre-registrations, runners, results files, void documents and checkers are
public, including the ones reporting failures, at
\url{https://github.com/vyakymenko/noophorics}. The metrics library is
dependency-free. Every number in this paper is traceable to a committed results
file, and the commit preceding each result is its registration.

\section*{On the prior-art claims in this paper}

The repository maintains a ledger (\texttt{theory/prior-art.md}) recording, for
each source, whether the primary text or only the abstract was read, and what may
therefore be claimed from it. Two constraints from that ledger apply here.
Endsley~\cite{endsley2020} is cited from its abstract and supports only that the
two measures diverge; \textbf{no pooled effect size is quoted}.
Newton~\cite{newton1990} is an unpublished doctoral dissertation with no journal,
volume or pages.

We also record what the human literature does \emph{not} license. None of these
studies elicited a rate comparable to our $\Phi$ from both parties against a
shared probe measure, and none involves language models in either role. The
resemblance is of shape, not of quantity.
